## C. Current thesis

Google does not publish a separate “AI SEO” recipe, a special AI schema, or a
guaranteed way to earn a source link in AI Overviews or AI Mode. A page can be
eligible as a supporting link when it is indexed and eligible for a Search
snippet; the durable work is the same foundation: useful, original,
people-first material; transparent authorship; crawlability; accurate metadata;
and structured data that reflects visible content. Measure actual visibility and
outcomes in Search Console and Analytics, but do not present an AI-link
appearance as a stable ranking score or a promised result.

## What survives from the 2025 material

### Still directionally current — rewrite with evidence

- Google Search contains AI Overviews and AI Mode, which can surface supporting
  web links.
- Technical eligibility, indexability, readable content and sound conventional
  SEO remain prerequisites, not an obsolete layer replaced by AI.
- A useful, logically organized answer for a real reader remains a good editorial
  practice.
- Search Console and Analytics are relevant to measuring search outcomes.
- A publisher can use preview controls when it needs to restrict material from
  being used in Google Search previews and AI features.

### Requires material update

- **AI Overview “(SGE)” and feature description:** use current product names;
  explain that AI Overviews and AI Mode may use different models and techniques,
  so links and responses vary. AI Overviews do not appear for every query.
- **Source links / “AI Citations”:** call them supporting links or source
  visibility, not a new universal SEO score. Google supplies no public,
  site-level “AI Citation” ranking metric or selection formula.
- **“Prompt SEO”:** distinguish a useful writing workflow from a supposed Google
  optimization discipline. Google explicitly rejects creating pages for every
  query or fan-out variation to manipulate AI responses.
- **Gemini mechanism:** remove claims such as “prompt ranking”, a fixed source
  choice sequence, or a particular Gemini decision process. Google documents
  query fan-out as a Search technique, not a lever publishers can optimize by
  producing one page per generated subquery.
- **Search Console:** the old “no AI Overview report” statement is obsolete.
  Overall AI-feature traffic remains within Web performance data; Google also
  announced dedicated generative-AI performance views and said they reached all
  sites on 2026-08-31. Explain fields and limitations, not a made-up citation
  score.
- **Visibility controls:** `nosnippet`, `max-snippet`, `data-nosnippet`, and
  `noindex` affect Search presentation and direct AI-feature input as documented.
  `Google-Extended` is for training/grounding in certain *other* Google systems;
  it is not the control to remove a page from AI Overviews or AI Mode.
- **E-E-A-T:** present it as a useful quality framework and a set of assessed
  characteristics, not as a single direct ranking factor or an AI-link trigger.
  Trust is the most important aspect in Google's description.
- **CTR impact:** delete universal percentages and country claims unless a later
  article introduces a clearly bounded, reproducible dataset. Query, feature,
  device, country and intent vary. Measure the site’s own impressions, clicks,
  conversions and engagement.
- **Structured data / metadata:** accurate titles, descriptions, alt text and
  markup matter as content quality and Search-appearance inputs; none is an
  “AI Overview schema” or a guarantee of a source link.

### Missing from the old text

- AI Mode and the documented difference from AI Overviews.
- Query fan-out as an explanatory mechanism, with an explicit warning that it is
  not an invitation to manufacture a page for every sub-query.
- Official eligibility rule: a source-link page must be indexed and eligible for
  a Search snippet, but eligibility never guarantees crawling, indexing or
  serving.
- Current Search Console reporting: aggregate Web data plus the generative-AI
  views, and a measurement workflow that joins Search Console to Analytics.
- Preferred Sources: a reader-selected, domain/subdomain-level preference that
  may highlight a source in Top Stories, AI Mode and AI Overviews; it is not a
  generic AI-ranking signal.
- The current UI direction: Google has expanded inline links and experiments
  with source/publisher presentation. Treat these as product surfaces that can
  change, not a publisher promise.
- The people-first, authorship, “Who / How / Why”, disclosure and scaled-content
  abuse guidance for AI-assisted editorial work.
- Correct distinction between a feature-specific rich-result markup and a
  general content structure. FAQ rich results are deprecated; HowTo rich results
  have long been unavailable.
- A practical control checklist: indexability, snippet eligibility, preview
  directives, source attribution, accurate metadata and outcome measurement.

## D. Suggested structure for a future article

1. **What actually changed in Google Search in 2026**
   - Define AI Overviews, AI Mode and supporting links. State variability and
     lack of guarantees up front.
2. **The three myths to discard**
   - “AI SEO is a separate recipe”, “AI Citation is a ranking score”, and “one
     topic = one page = one answer”.
3. **What makes a page eligible — and what it does not promise**
   - Indexed + snippet-eligible; fundamentals, policies and people-first
     material; no special AI markup.
4. **Create source-worthy work, not commodity rewrites**
   - Original experience, evidence, author/date, transparent corrections,
     practical examples, relevant images or video where useful.
5. **AI-assisted publication without scaled-content abuse**
   - The role of review, factual verification, provenance and metadata; why
     volume and keyword/query permutations are not a strategy.
6. **Metadata and structured data: the bounded role**
   - Accurate visible-to-user Article/Breadcrumb markup where applicable; titles,
     descriptions and alt text; no FAQ/HowTo shortcut.
7. **Links, attribution and Preferred Sources**
   - Supporting links, inline links, reader choice, domain-level preference and
     what a publisher can and cannot control.
8. **Measurement in Search Console and Analytics**
   - Generative-AI views, Web totals, pages/countries/devices/dates, conversion
     and engagement. Explain correlation limits.
9. **Content controls and edge cases**
   - `nosnippet`, `max-snippet`, `data-nosnippet`, `noindex`; explain the
     distinct purpose of Google-Extended.
10. **A restrained 2026 editorial checklist**
    - Five to seven verifiable decisions; no promise of inclusion or CTR uplift.

## F. New topics required for 2026

- AI Mode alongside AI Overviews, including its exploration/comparison use case.
- The distinction between **eligibility**, **appearance**, **source-link
  presentation**, **click**, and **business outcome**.
- Query fan-out without “fan-out content farms”.
- Search Console’s dedicated generative-AI visibility views and their analytical
  limits; pair them with Analytics rather than inferring causality from CTR.
- Publisher-side Preferred Sources implementation, only as a reader-choice and
  audience relationship feature.
- A precise content-control section: snippet controls versus Google-Extended.
- AI-assisted editorial governance: factual review, real authorship,
  documentation of material automation where readers would reasonably expect it,
  and protection against scaled-content abuse.
- Structured data’s correct, limited role; accurate metadata and visible content
  alignment.
- Feature and interface volatility: product announcements/experiments are dated
  observations, not evergreen ranking rules.

## H. Value for the Redactoria EDU / SEO cluster

**Yes, but only as a carefully bounded foundational SEO article.** It can teach a
beginner or small publisher how to separate controllable site work from claims
about opaque generative systems. That supports the EDU mission better than a
trend piece about “winning citations”.

It should sit in an SEO / technical publishing cluster and link conceptually to
content quality, structured data, crawlability, Search Console interpretation and
responsible AI-assisted writing. It should **not** promise traffic recovery,
citation acquisition, ranking uplift, or a permanent UI behavior. Because the
topic changes quickly, the final piece needs an explicit reviewed-on date and a
future revalidation trigger for Search Console and AI-feature documentation.
